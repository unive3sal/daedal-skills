# ETH recommendation-letter guidance: source notes

## Provenance and scope

- **Publisher:** ETH Zürich, Human Resources.
- **Document:** *Beispiel Letter of Reference*; footer: “ETH Human Resources Beispiel Letter of Reference, 04.09.2013.”
- **Date:** 4 September 2013; two pages.
- **Original PDF:** https://ethz.ch/content/dam/ethz/associates/services/Anstellung-Arbeiten/fuehrung-und-entwicklung/Beispiel_Letter_of_Reference.pdf
- **Consulted:** 26 November 2026.

This file summarizes the guide rather than reproducing its example letters. The guide concerns formal letters of reference/recommendation for employment and academic study. It is not an ETH admissions policy, a current legal standard for employment certificates, or a mandatory template for every institution. Follow actual recipient requirements when they differ.

## Source recommendations

### Formal letter and addressee — page 1

A letter of reference/recommendation testifies to an individual's skills, personal characteristics, and work. It is a formal document with an addressee. Address a known recipient by name. For a committee, the guide gives “Dear hiring committee”; when the addressee is unknown, it gives “To whom it may concern” or “Dear Sir or Madam.”

The skill uses the relevant committee name and avoids assuming gendered titles. These are adaptations, not additional quotations from ETH.

### Introduction — page 1

Express the writer's basic opinion of the person's skills, work, and characteristics in the first sentence. Identify the purpose in the opening section, for example academic study or employment.

ETH labels the following illustrative opening phrases by strength:

| Strength label in the guide | Example opening |
| --- | --- |
| Very low | “This letter is in reference to…” |
| Fairly low | “This serves as a letter of reference for…” |
| Average | “I am pleased to write this letter of recommendation for…” |
| Fairly high | “I am pleased to recommend…” |
| Very high | “It is a genuine pleasure and honor for me to recommend…” |

Spelling and capitalization have been normalized. Treat these as examples of endorsement signals in this guide, not a universal phrase-scoring rubric. The broader letter and its supporting evidence determine credibility.

### Position and context — page 1

State the writer's relationship to the applicant, their relative position, and how long they have known or worked with the person. The source examples concern thesis supervision and research recruitment; those example identities and achievements are not facts to reuse.

### Overall assessment — pages 1–2

- Use **2–6 paragraphs**, depending on the length and closeness of the working relationship.
- Begin with a general overview of skills, potential, personal characteristics, and behavior.
- Focus on the intended job or academic course of study.
- Describe specific circumstances demonstrating the previously mentioned skills and characteristics.
- Highlight the most relevant advantages and cover both technical and social skills.
- Be as specific as possible.

The sample phrases illustrate scientific curiosity, practical problem-solving, independence and teamwork, diligence, managing concurrent activities, breaking problems into components, quantitative rigor, command of relevant literature, seeking advice, sharing knowledge, project management, and technical expertise. These are illustrative dimensions, not a required checklist of attributes for every applicant.

### Conclusion — page 2

Briefly summarize why the writer recommends the person and which skills equip them for the desired post. Express a clear recommendation. The source gives examples such as “I highly recommend this person” and “I recommend without hesitation.”

Some sample conclusions predict academic success; they are examples, not a requirement to make extravagant predictions or promise outcomes.

### Closing remarks — page 2

Offer additional information if required, including a willingness to answer specific questions or provide further details.

## Additional safeguards introduced by this skill

The following are implementation choices for trustworthy drafting, not claims that the PDF explicitly contains them:

- Gather the brief efficiently; use labeled placeholders instead of fabricated facts when information is missing.
- Distinguish direct observation from CV material and applicant-reported facts.
- Preserve contribution scope, numbers, dates, comparison bases, publication status, and endorsement strength.
- Use the applicant's stated pronouns or neutral language, and avoid unnecessary sensitive personal information.
- Mark applicant-prepared text as a draft for recommender approval; do not forge signatures or imply prior approval.
- Keep private materials private and require explicit authorization before external sharing or submission.
- Default to concise, copy-ready prose and adapt language, salutation, and length to the actual recipient.
- Treat approximately one page as an optional practical starting point. The ETH guide does **not** specify a total word count, a one-page limit, a required file format, or a universal font/layout.

Do not insert this source citation into an applicant's letter unless the user specifically requests it. The guide informs the writing process; it is not evidence about the applicant.
